# C7 — Morpheme Stacking (English)

This test exercises the morpheme stacking mechanism borrowed from
Ithkuil's morphological slot system. Any term can carry a chain of
suffixes selecting aspect, modality, certainty, and scope values,
with each slot drawn from a fixed vocabulary.

## Aspect alone

The aspect slot carries tense-and-phase information. We show terms
with progressive aspect (something is in progress), perfective aspect
(something has completed), iterative aspect (something is repeated),
inchoative aspect (something is beginning), and terminative aspect
(something is ending).

## Modality alone

The modality slot carries obligation or permission information. We
show terms marked as must (obligatory), may (permitted), or cant
(impossible).

## Certainty alone

The certainty slot carries epistemic information. We show terms
marked as certain, probable, or possible.

## Scope alone

The scope slot carries scope-of-effect information. We show terms
marked as local, global, or context-dependent.

## Two-element stacks

Combinations of two morpheme slots begin to convey richer meaning.
A term that is progressive and certain means something is definitely
happening now. A term that is inchoative and obligatory means
something must begin. A term that is iterative and permitted means
something may happen repeatedly.

## Three-element stacks

Three-slot stacks express more nuance. Progressive-probable-local
means something is probably happening now in a local scope.
Perfective-obligatory-global means something must have completed, at
global scope.

## Four-element stacks

Four-slot stacks approach the density limit of the notation. Iterative-
permitted-doubtful-contextual means something might repeatedly happen,
doubtfully, depending on context.

## Five-element stacks

Five-slot stacks are the maximum the notation supports. Progressive-
permitted-possibly-local means something may possibly be happening
now in a local scope. In English this concept takes nine words; in
CSLv3 it is six tokens.
